Lost Your Job? Your Two Priorities for the First Few Weeks

Nishant Gupta · 6 min read

So you've lost your job and you're wondering what to do next. Once the salary stops, two things matter more than anything else: finding your next source of income as quickly as possible, and making your emergency fund last as long as possible.

Decide what you actually want next

The fastest way to a new job is through your network. But before you start calling people, take a moment to think. Do you want the same kind of job you've done for years? Or is this the time to try the business idea or passion you've been sitting on?

Here are the options I listed for myself:

  • A full-time job at a similar or higher role and pay
  • A full-time job at a similar or lower role, with a pay cut
  • A job in something you're passionate about, which may pay less because you lack experience in it
  • Your own business, or a franchise
  • Freelancing, using your contacts and experience
  • Turning a hobby or side project into income
  • Becoming an insurance or mutual fund agent, if you're good with people
  • Affiliate marketing, if you have a social following
  • Part-time or contract work, in your field or with gig platforms
  • Actually retiring, if you believe in FIRE and have the corpus for it

My suggestion: don't close any of these doors, but put your energy into the two or three you think can work.

Week one: your resume and your network

Spend the first week on your resume. If you're applying for different kinds of roles, make a version for each instead of one generic CV. Update your job-portal profiles and LinkedIn, set up job alerts, and let people know you're looking.

Then reach out: old colleagues, former managers, classmates, neighbours, friends. At mid or senior level, many of them will be well settled in their companies. Tell them what happened and ask directly for referrals. Most people will want to help.

Weeks one and two: your money

Sit down and take stock. How big is your emergency fund? What insurance do you have now that the office cover is gone? How much have you saved for future goals?

Then write down your monthly expenses in three buckets: essentials, non-essentials, and SIPs for future goals. Some costs will fall now that you're not commuting, like fuel and eating out. From this you can work out how many months your emergency fund will last.

Don't leave that fund sitting in a savings account earning 2–3%. Move it into fixed deposits or short-term debt funds, where it earns more but stays easy to access.

Making the severance last

Many companies pay notice-period salary plus severance on a layoff, often one month's pay for each year of service. Say you worked at the company for 3 years, earned ₹2 lakh a month and had a two-month notice period: that's ₹6 lakh of severance plus ₹4 lakh of notice pay, ₹10 lakh in total before tax.

One way to use a lump sum like this is to put it in a hybrid or debt fund and set up a Systematic Withdrawal Plan (SWP) for when your emergency fund runs out. On ₹10 lakh earning about 10% a year, withdrawing ₹50,000 a month would last roughly 21–22 months. That's close to two more years to find your feet.

The short version

  • Decide what you actually want to do next
  • Make resume variants and share them widely in your network
  • Tell people you've been laid off, and ask for help
  • Review your finances and move your emergency fund into low-risk, easy-to-access options
  • Invest your severance and plan an SWP for when the emergency fund runs out
